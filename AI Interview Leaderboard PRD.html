1. Overview

The AI Interview leaderboard ranks candidates for a position by their AI-evaluated interview performance and lets recruiters act on the results without leaving the page. It lives in the AI Interview Center within Talent Acquisition, scoped to one position and one interview round at a time. Recruiters can narrow the list with natural-language filters, review AI-generated evidence per hiring criterion, watch interview highlights, ask the AI questions about a candidate, and advance or share candidates individually or in bulk.

2. Problem

After AI interviews are scored, recruiters review candidates one profile at a time. There is no ranked, comparative view of a position's interview outcomes, no way to slice candidates by behavioral signals ("strong problem solvers who passed all coding tests"), and no path from insight to action — advancing a candidate requires navigating to a separate pipeline surface. Review is slow and the strongest candidates are not obvious.

3. Goals and non-goals

Goals

  • Give recruiters a ranked, evidence-backed view of scored interviews for a position.
  • Make every ranking explainable: verdicts, per-criterion scores, transcript evidence, timestamps.
  • Let recruiters filter with natural language and understand how the AI curated the results.
  • Close the loop: advance stage and share with hiring manager directly from the leaderboard, singly or in bulk, with undo.

Non-goals

  • Replacing the full candidate profile, interview scheduling, or feedback collection.
  • Side-by-side candidate comparison (deferred — see §9).
  • Authoring or tuning the interview scoring model itself.

4. Users

  • Recruiter (primary) — owns the pipeline for a requisition; reviews scored interviews, decides who moves forward.
  • Hiring manager (secondary) — receives shared candidates; reviews evidence and returns a verdict per candidate (§5-G), but does not operate the pipeline.

A.1 Email entry point

When it sends. Digest-style, not per interview — one email per position per trigger, deduplicated within 24h:

  • First results — the first 5 interviews for a round are scored ("Your first scored interviews are in").
  • Batch complete — all scheduled interviews for a round are scored.
  • Standout candidate — a new score lands in the Strong-hire band (≥4.3) while the position is open.
  • Going stale — strong candidates have sat unactioned for 5 business days ("3 strong candidates are waiting on you").

What's in it (the nudge). Enough signal to earn the click, not enough to decide from the inbox:

  • Subject names the position and the headline: "UX Designer · Top 5 candidates scored 4.3+".
  • Top 3 candidates — name, score, one-line AI verdict — no evidence, no video.
  • One stat line: interviews scored vs scheduled, batch average.
  • Single CTA "View leaderboard", deep-linked to the position and round; standout emails link with that candidate pre-selected in the rail.
  • No per-candidate actions in the email — advancing requires the evidence view by design.

G. Hiring manager — reviewing shared candidates

Sharing creates a review request. The HM works from a read-only view of the same evidence; the recruiter keeps pipeline control.

IDUser storyAcceptance criteria

G2As a hiring manager, I want to see the shared candidates only — not the whole pipeline — so that my review is focused.CTA opens a shared-with-you view: the leaderboard filtered to the shared set, read-only (no advance, no bulk, no filter over unshared candidates).
G3As a hiring manager, I want the same evidence the recruiter saw, so that I can judge independently.Full rail per candidate: verdict, criteria with evidence and timestamps, highlight reel, Ask AI.

G5As a recruiter, I want to see HM verdicts on the leaderboard, so that I can act on them without a meeting.Shared candidates show an HM-review status chip (Waiting / Endorsed / Passed / Discuss) on the card and rail; notification to the recruiter when all verdicts are in; endorsed candidates surface "Advance stage" as the suggested next action.
G6As a hiring manager, I want to be nudged if a review request sits idle, so that candidates don't go cold on my desk.One reminder email after 3 business days; recruiter sees "waiting N days" on the status chip.

6. Functional notes

  • Scoring model. Overall score is a weighted blend of five criteria: Problem solving 30%, Coding proficiency 25%, Communication 20%, Design craft 15%, Collaboration 10%. Criteria can be unassessed (e.g. skipped case study) and are excluded rather than zeroed.
  • Verdict bands. Strong hire signal / Moderate / Below bar, derived from score and flags; verdict copy references the batch average.
  • Empty states. Filter with no matches shows an explanation and a clear-filter action; no selection shows rail guidance.
  • Responsive. Rail narrows at ≤1240px and hides at ≤860px (open question §10.2); card actions compress labels at ≤1080px.
  • Motion. Ease-out, 120–520ms; all shimmer and slide animations respect prefers-reduced-motion.

7. Solution options — post-filter guidance

Three working variants exist for what the AI shows after a natural-language filter runs. A decision is needed before build.

Option 1 — Rationale line only v6 · baseline

Beneath the AI-written result title, one line explains what was read, how evidence was weighted, and who was excluded.

  • Lowest visual cost; keeps the list dense.
  • Explains the past query but doesn't help compose the next one.

Option 2 — Contextual follow-up prompts v6.1 · proof of concept

After each search, the AI regenerates follow-up prompt chips ("Of these, who led a team?") that chain into the input. Prompt skeletons load after the list settles.

  • Teaches the query language; encourages iterative narrowing.
  • More vertical space and more AI calls; prompts risk feeling generic.

Option 3 — Inline justification v6.2 · proof of concept

The filter card itself shows a single justification statement — parameters applied, criteria used, weights — with no subtext under the title.

  • Strongest transparency story; audit-friendly.
  • No forward guidance; densest copy of the three.

Recommendation: pending usability read-out. Options 2 and 3 could combine (justification in the card, prompts under the list) at extra cost.

8. Alternatives explored for the overall layout

Earlier prototypes tested other shapes for the same job; v6 was selected for lowest interaction cost.

  • v3 Guided — split "fresh vs acted upon" lists with inline highlights.
  • v3.1 Action history — single leaderboard with an actions log on top.
  • v4 Refined — v3 with hierarchy, copy, and state fixes from audit.
  • v5 Chat — conversational AI panel driving a live leaderboard.
  • v6 Simplified (chosen) — cards plus one persistent detail rail; Ask moved inline.

9. Out of scope / deferred

  • Compare candidates. "Add to compare" was removed pending a compare-view design.
  • Candidate profile page. Linked from the rail but mocked; needs backend wiring.
  • Live AI backend. Prototype filter/ask responses are canned; production requires the interview-intelligence service.
  • Video playback. Reel modal is a stub; production uses the interview recording service with timestamp deep-links.

10. Open questions

  1. Which post-filter guidance option (§7) ships first?
  2. Email cadence: are the four triggers in §5-A.1 right, and should recruiters be able to mute per position?
  3. HM review (§5-G): does a Thumbs down remove the candidate from the recruiter's default view, or only mark them?
  4. Narrow viewports: keep hiding the rail below ~900px, or overlay it above the list?
  5. Should "Share with HM" collect a note, or share silently as today?
  6. Does the HM shared view (§5-G2) reuse this page with a role gate, or ship as a separate lightweight surface?

11. Success metrics

  • Time from "scores ready" to first pipeline action, vs current profile-by-profile flow.
  • Share of advances executed from the leaderboard (target: majority within a quarter).
  • Filter adoption: % of sessions using natural-language filter; follow-up query rate.
  • Undo rate below 5% of actions (proxy for decision confidence).
