Vamsi Vinta explained that the feature incorporates a retrieval-augmented generation system over transcripts, enabling recruiters to query transcript evidence rather than reading through every document.
Success Metrics for AI Interviews: Ruta Srinivasaraju Raju and Deepali Saxena discussed the omission of primary success metrics in the initial product requirements document. Deepali Saxena noted that time to hire for candidates undergoing AI interviews currently lags behind others because clear next steps are often missing after an interview concludes.
Entry Points and Access Points for the Shortlist: Aritro Saha presented the planned entry points for the AI interview shortlist, which include pipeline tickers, emails, Slack messages, and an automation live toolbar or briefcase icon. Ruta Srinivasaraju Raju and Deepali Saxena evaluated the automation banner life cycle, suggesting that when automation is declined, the banner should be repurposed to welcome recruiters with newly available candidate shortlists instead of displaying an unselected automation prompt.

Navigation and Interface Architecture Concerns: Ruta Srinivasaraju Raju expressed concern over adding new top-level navigation links or separate pages, suggesting instead that entry points should reside on position overview pages or the Talent homepage. Deepali Saxena noted that the Talent pilot is at least six months away from general availability, and recruiters primarily operate at the individual position level rather than the general homepage.
Shortlist Page Layout and AI Filtering Design: Aritro Saha demonstrated the AI interview shortlist interface, showing how it features a position switcher, interview switcher, and a default view displaying the top 10 candidates ranked by interview score. Aritro Saha explained that their design utilizes AI filtering options at the top, incorporating static buckets such as candidates scoring four or more overall alongside dynamic, role-specific contextual buckets.

Debate Over Dedicated Page Versus Pipeline Tab: Ruta Srinivasaraju Raju questioned the need for a completely new page, arguing that the shortlist features should instead be integrated as a dedicated tab within the existing pipeline page to maintain a consistent design language. Deepali Saxena and Aritro Saha defended the separate page approach, citing pipeline page clutter, limitations in current user interface elements, and future scalability for handling technical issues and incomplete interviews.
Technical Constraints of Applicant Tracking System Integration: Akhilesh Goyal and Aritro Saha explained that adding a new tab to the pipeline page introduces complexities because tabs map directly to applicant tracking system stages, which can range from 20 to 40 per customer and are managed using Eightfold stage groups. Akhilesh Goyal noted that while implementation is technically possible, customer-specific setup customizations require careful management.
Resolution on Engineering Exploration and Core Problem Statement: Vamsi Vinta and Ruta Srinivasaraju Raju agreed to consult with engineering to determine whether shortlist elements can be embedded natively within the pipeline page without requiring a separate page or iframe. Deepali Saxena concluded by outlining the core problem statement, stating that recruiters feel lost on a cluttered pipeline page when trying to quickly identify and prioritize candidates who have completed interviews within a limited timeframe.

Candidate Pipeline View and High-Volume Scalability: Deepali Saxena raised a problem statement regarding the existing pipeline page, noting it functions merely as a flat list of candidates rather than prioritizing individuals who need attention after completing an AI interview. Ruta Srinivasaraju Raju presented an alternative tab view that narrows candidates to those who have completed AI interviews, sorts them by score, and displays detailed information in a tabular layout. Deepali questioned whether a separate tab was necessary if similar capabilities could be built directly into the main pipeline page. Ruta argued that card-based views do not scale for high-volume hiring environments like Mercato Libre, where hundreds or thousands of candidates are handled daily, whereas tables allow for better sorting, bulk actions, and data exporting. Aritro Saha expressed concern that excessive configurability might clutter the interface with too much information. Ultimately, Deepali agreed that high-volume use cases must be accommodated while maintaining a simple default enterprise experience.
Candidate Profile and Multi-Round Interview Context: The team debated whether selecting a candidate should display a single isolated interview context (such as a 360 interview) or an overview encompassing all interview rounds. Ruta and Deepali argued that recruiters require comprehensive context across multiple interview rounds to make informed hiring decisions, rendering a single-interview limitation ineffective. Aritro initially suggested focusing on one interview at a time based on sequential AI interview steps, but Deepali and Ruta pointed out that interview sequences vary by position and are not always standardized. Deepali concluded that the team should explore utilizing the existing pipeline page while integrating new elements—such as chat functionality and targeted filters—and ensuring that candidate details display scores across all interview rounds.
Iteration Speed and Engineering Feasibility for Pipeline Enhancements: Ruta suggested expediting development by adding an AI filter and search directly onto the existing table rather than introducing an entirely new design pattern or tab. Aritro raised concerns regarding engineering complexity, configurability, and whether such features could be built within a short timeframe. Deepali requested faster iteration and concept alignment within the week. Ruta referenced previously developed internal concepts, including copilot tools worked on by Kartikeya and AI search features explored by Kora, to avoid redundant design efforts. Aritro committed to verifying engineering feasibility, checking the status of AI search features, and presenting a viable concept matching the problem statement during the week.
